# Trending film, animation and anime moods: 20 proposed theme palettes

Research date: 2026-11-16. Every source below was read on 2026-11-16 unless a different date is given.
No images or files were downloaded; colour moods come from text on official pages, trade coverage and reviews.

The title names in this file are **for our notes only**. They must never appear in the app, in theme names, in the store listing or in screenshots. A theme ships as a name and 10 hex colours, nothing else.

## Summary

- Netflix's own Top 10 (latest published week **2–8 November 2026**), the US weekend charts for **6–8 and 13–15 November 2026**, the Japan box office and the anime TV ratings give about 23 titles across live action, animated films, cartoon series and anime.
- Netflix does **not** publish a Myanmar Top 10. Its country selector lists Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam, so those are used as the nearest regional signal. The anime titles that chart in Vietnam and Malaysia (*The Apothecary Diaries* S3, *Firefly Wedding*, *Dragon Ball Super: Beerus*) and the Annecy winner from Singapore (*The Violinist*) are weighted for that reason.
- 20 original palettes are proposed: 10 light and 10 dark. All 20 pass every contrast floor in a Python check, and none is near-identical to another or to the existing 10 (Part 3).
- Colour itself is not protected by copyright, and a single colour is never inherently distinctive as a US trademark. Names and artwork are the risk, so every name is a plain English word that does not echo a title (Part 4).

- **Names rejected on the way:**
  - "Clay": too close to *Clayface*.
  - "Firefly": part of *Firefly Wedding*.
  - "Glimmer": also a cartoon character name.
  - "Nebula": also a comic-film character name.
  - "Gaslight": unwanted connotation.
- **The inspiring title** is for these notes only and is never shown in the app.
- **Colour order:** background, key, keyPressed, actionKey, label, actionLabel, hint, callout, accent, onAccent.

### Verification

Checked with a Python script, kept outside the repo in the session scratchpad. It uses the WCAG 2 relative luminance with sRGB linearisation (c ≤ 0.03928 ? c/12.92 : ((c+0.055)/1.055)^2.4; L = 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B; ratio = (L1+0.05)/(L2+0.05)).

The script checked:

- **The contrast floors:**
  - label/key, label/keyPressed, label/callout, actionLabel/actionKey, hint/key and onAccent/accent: 4.5 or more.
  - accent/background: 3.0 or more.
  - keyPressed/key: 1.5 or more.
- **Key against background:** above 1.05, so the keys stand apart from the background.
- **Light or dark:** a light theme's background must contrast less with white than with black, and a dark theme's the reverse.
- **Near-identical palettes:** a pair counts as near-identical when the background ΔE2000 is under 5, or when the mean ΔE2000 over background, key, actionKey and accent is under 10. The check runs against all 19 other new themes and the 10 existing ones.

**Result: all 20 pass.** Every theme has label/key of 9.67 or more, well above the 6 target for small Myanmar marks.

## Part 4: IP notes (factual, not legal advice)

- **Google Play, Intellectual Property policy** (Play Console Help, "Intellectual Property": https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888072).
  - Apps must not infringe trademarks, meaning unauthorised use of an identical or similar mark that is likely to cause confusion.
  - Apps must not use another party's brand names or logos without permission.
  - The policy lists, as common copyright violations, marketing images from movies or TV and artwork or images from cartoons, comics, films or TV.
  - The policy points developers to original content as the safe route.

  For us, this means no film, character or studio names in theme names, the store listing or screenshots, and no stills, logos or character art.
- **US copyright, words and colouring:** 37 CFR § 202.1(a) lists material not subject to copyright. It includes words and short phrases such as names, titles and slogans, and "mere variations of typographic ornamentation, lettering or coloring". Source: Cornell LII copy of the regulation, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/202.1. The US Copyright Office explains the same rule in Circular 33, *Works Not Protected by Copyright*: https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf. That file is a PDF whose text I could not extract, so the regulation above is the text I relied on.

  In practice, a palette of 10 hex colours is not a copyrightable work in the US. A film's artwork is, so we take no images.
- **US trademark, words and colour:**
  - The USPTO says a trademark can be any word, phrase, symbol or design, or a combination, that identifies the source of goods or services (https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/what-trademark).
  - USPTO examination guidance (TMEP § 1202.05(a), "Color Marks Never Inherently Distinctive") requires proof of acquired distinctiveness before a colour alone can be registered. A USPTO slide deck on non-traditional marks says the same: https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/tm-non-traditional-marks-20251216.pdf. I saw this through a search summary and did not open the PDF.

  So the realistic trademark risk is in **names**, not in hex values. Hence the plain-word names.
- **Not covered:** Myanmar and other national laws were not researched. Treat this section as background for the owner, not as a clearance opinion.
